<span id="page-142-0"></span>Familiars are artificial creatures and constructs that slavishly follow and attend to their master. These are generally restricted to creatures smaller than an adult human (anything else would class as a servitor), and by their nature cleave to a single master unless instructed otherwise. Mostly, familiars do not possess any significant degree of intelligence and are trained or programmed to perform certain tasks on command. Familiars are either living creatures controlled by the mind of their master via arcane psyber neuro-implants or are cybernetic creatures controlled by voice or coded vox-signal command. Such slaved creatures are the strange fruit of ancient sciences and arcane superstitions, and the ownership and command of familiars utilise many technologies that the Machine Cult considers sacred. A trailing throng of bonded familiars is a sign of status both within the Mechanicus and the Imperium at large.
## **Controlling Familiars**

In many respects, familiars function as any other creature would. They have Characteristics, Skills, Talents, Traits, and so forth. However, because of their servitude to their controller, they possess several special rules and considerations.

Familiars are controlled by their masters through the use of simple commands. These are generally single words, short sentences or the equivalent, and so giving a command is generally a Free Action. At the GM's discretion, the familiar may have to succeed on an Intelligence Test to interpret a contradictory or complex order. Should a master come under attack, and the familiar is aware of the transgression against its master, it will automatically move to its master's defence, attacking the assailant unless it receives specific instruction (or possesses an instinct) to the contrary. Upon completing a task, the familiar returns to its master's side unless it's received prior instructions.

Depending upon the nature of the familiar's construction and the level of techno-augmentation it has undergone it may or may not still possess any instincts that a normal creature of its type would ordinarily have—see the Cyber-Creatures section below for further information.
## **Creating a Familiar**

Familiars can be based on a wide number of living creatures, bio-constructs, or pure automata. Once a suitable "host" creature has been selected it can be chirurgically and technologically modified into a familiar by applying one or both of the following modifications. 

Servo-skulls and cherubim are very particular kinds of familiar common to the Adepta of the Imperium, and are dealt with separately. Likewise, although there is considerable overlap between them, the process of creating a servitor has some significant fundamental differences and is not discussed here.
### **Cyber-Creature**

Cyber-familiars are semi-intelligent machine versions or cybernetic adaptations of living creatures controlled by a master, loyalty to whom is hardwired in the creature's brain. Some cyber familiars are created as assistants to serve prominent adepts and the Mechanicus, or merely as amusements for the wealthy, while others are fitted for war with implanted weapon systems and all manner of augmetics. The canine cyber-mastiffs of the Adeptus Arbites and more elite enforcer cadres are undoubtedly the most common and best known type of cyber-creature, although a great many beasts could conceivably become a cyber-creature for a master with the requisite funds and interest. 
### **Psy-Bonded Familiar**

A psy-bonded (or "psyber") familiar is a living creature bonded to its master by arcane technology. Interface circuitry in both familiar and master (called a psyber-lure) allows the creature to be directly and precisely controlled, with the master having access to the sensory experiences of the creature and able to give it instantaneous commands at significant distance. Although commonly thought of as a novel plaything for the wealthy, a psyber-familiar can be a powerful ally for any agent that requires heightened awareness of their surroundings or a subtle method of surveiling the area. Owing to the idiosyncrasies of this poorly-understood tech-artifice usually only small and non-sapient creatures can become psy-bonded.
### **Servo-Skull**

Servo-skulls are human skulls, often that of a favoured servant or low ranking tech-priest, that have been fitted with rudimentary machine-spirits, support systems, and a small anti-grav motor to provide lift. These constructs are devoted to a particular task and have the basic equipment to carry out the purpose that they have been programmed to perform. A servo-skull's small size and manoeuvrability allows it to enter otherwise inaccessible spaces or hazardous areas that would endanger their operators, as well as the ability to act as additional "eyes and ears" for their masters. The servo-skull is synonymous with the Adeptus Mechanicus, who regard these creations as excellent servants and their machine-spirits as exceptionally faithful and pure. Servo-skulls can range from uncommon to nearly ubiquitous on many developed worlds in the Imperium, where they are often found serving with the other Adepta and most organisations with reasonably deep coffers.
#### Servo-Skulls as Familiars
A servo-skull can be used much like a voice-controlled cyber-familiar and will follow its master's commands to the best of its ability. All servo-skulls can hover a few metres above the ground and may remain stationary for any length of time. A servo-skull's power cells will generally provide enough power for several days of operation before it must be recharged. Servo-skulls can perform various functions based on their design, with more advanced (and costly) examples capable of a greater range of abilities.

This cyber-mastiff is typical of those encountered in the kennels of better-funded enforcer cadres, but might also be found in the employ of bounty hunters, well-heeled ganger heavies, and as literal watchdogs on noble estates. It still possesses much of the flesh it was born with and consequently acts not entirely differently than an ordinary Terran hound might, but the obvious dermal plating and servo-reinforced jaw would quickly dissuade any would-be adversary from thinking they can dispatch it with a few kicks. In spite of its augments, this "basic" cyber-mastiff retains enough of its animal instincts to act independently when allowed to do so, and could even serve as a companion to its master if they so desired.

#### **"Bullpup" Cyber-Mastiff**
An extremely heavy-duty cyber-mastiff design, the Bullpup was originally commissioned by a Lord Marshal of the Adeptus Arbites on Landunder's Hive Subrique as a pure terror weapon for suppressing riots and breaking up ganger dens. This beast of a cyber-construct is manufactured using a sabre-wolf, an apex predator from the notorious feral world of Dusk, which is then heavily augmented into a powerful construct that can rival many combat servitors in terms of both strength and durability. The sabre-wolf's already sturdy frame is largely encased in dermal armour with reinforced exoskeletal augmetics and synth-muscle, resulting in a massively built techno-beast of considerable bulk that could even bring down an ogryn if need be. Bullpups are equipped with shock jaws for use in incapacitating wanted targets and witnesses, and are also uniquely fitted with a magnetic locking system in their feet and neck to very firmly restrain a suspect until the Arbitrators arrive. The sabre-wolf's already terrifying howl is further augmented with a subsonic vox-hailer that emits low-frequency vocalisations that instil dread in anyone who hears it. Owing to the enormous amount of augmetics used, not to mention the expense of captive-raising sabre-wolves to serve as a base, Bullpups are issued to precincts sparingly and are only encountered with any frequency in locations with a high potential for civil unrest or a consistent history thereof.

#### **"Bloodhound" Hardcase Cyber-Mastiff**
Lord Sector Marius Hax once described a Bloodhound cyber-mastiff as "resembling a dog as seen by a tech-priest". Lithe and angular, Bloodhounds are constructed with an entirely metallic chassis with four legs and a head that follow the general shape of a canine but only bear the slightest passing resemblance to one. Armed with some of the most sophisticated olfactory sensors available in the entire sector and programmed with exhaustive tracking routines, Bloodhounds have unnaturally adroit perception that can humble even some purpose-built spoor tracker auspex. Part of the Bloodhound's insidiously effective programming includes a cortical implant that stimulates the cyber-mastiff's pleasure centres as it pursues a target, often causing them to become absolutely obsessive and relentless in their duties. Its relatively slim and narrow build further enables the Bloodhound to squeeze into tight spaces that a flesh and blood canine could not, leaving its quarry with nowhere to hide.

#### **Hardcase Eliminator-Mastiff**
Depending on the particulars of their construction, cyber-mastiffs can serve many roles and purposes for their master. The eliminator-mastiff, thought to have been originally devised by Malfian enforcers not long after the conclusion of the Angevin Crusade, is not one of these constructs. As its name suggests, the eliminator-mastiff is a true monotask construct: it ruthlessly tracks down and destroys its target. In combat, an eliminator-mastiff can be instructed to attack a given enemy, whereby it will launch itself like a logis-guided missile at the unfortunate victim and rend them to shreds with the in-built chainblades that make up its mandibles. A victim that tries to flee or manages to put distance or obstacles between himself and the eliminator-mastiff will find himself cut down at range by a pair of mounted lasguns in the construct's shoulders. More sinisterly—and entirely befitting the generally amoral and conniving nature of Malfian society—the eliminator-mastiff can also be provided with data regarding the target, such as a pict, spoor or gene sample, or even a vox recording. The eliminator-mastiff will then aggregate the available data into a composite profile of its target and set out to autonomously search and murder them, typically returning with some shredded bits of flesh still clinging to its jaws as proof of the successful hunt for its masters. This complicated system stack and its attendant data probes leave no room for protocols or programming to subdue or otherwise apprehend its target, and its utterly malicious machine spirit would rebel against such constraints anyway.

### **Grapplehawk**
This is a catch-all term for a class of avian cyber-constructs capable of harassing, subduing, and potentially even capturing a target at their master's direction. Typically rendered in a gleaming alloy chassis, a grapplehawk takes to the air with powerful suspensors providing much of its lift and propulsion—the creature's actual wings are largely for manoeuvring purposes only. The glittering crania contains the brain and nervous system of an appropriate raptor or some other bird of prey, transferring its natural hunting drive and ability to accurately target and pounce on a quarry from above into an entirely more dangerous body. Most grapplehawks appear as the name implies, taking the shape of a glossy metallic hawk or eagle, although owls, bats, and other flying hunters are occasionally modelled instead (typically by more eccentric commissioners or those who represent an organisation to whom the creature in question has special meaning).
#### **Helica-pattern Grapplehawk**
The Helica-pattern Grapplehawk is a representative example of "smaller" constructs that are compact enough to be carried on the person and easily deployed to pursue a fleeing target. A favourite of Calixian Arbitrators, the Helica-pattern has several design considerations that allow it to tuck its wings and neck in close to its body, resembling a large medallion or a perhaps a metallic satchel when at rest. On command, the Arbitrator fortunate enough to be issued such an artifice can launch the grapplehawk to chase down a miscreant, and most will often give the construct a boost by hurling it into the air towards the target whilst issuing the order to seek. The grapplehawk will then streak through the air, easily avoiding ground-based obstacles (including crowds of rioters) before zeroing in on its target and violently heckling them with its shock pulse claws. Even if the target is able to avoid being incapacitated by the shocking talon strikes, the gleaming grapplehawk's chassis and angrily flapping wings easily identify the target so its master can better pursue them.

#### **Falax-pattern Grapplehawk**
This heavyweight grapplehawk is more of a weapon than a tool, able to use its great speed and powerful talons to rapidly chase down a target and begin rending it to pieces. Over half the height of a man and with a wingspan greater than three metres, a brace of powerful suspensors give the Falax-pattern frightening speed in a dive and a surprising ability to heft significant weight aloft. Armed with these abilities, this construct specialises in crashing into its target and pinning it to the ground where it can tear the unfortunate victim apart with its talons. Alternatively, the grapplehawk can utilise its oversized suspensors to carry its target into the air, either to return the target to its master, or to simply let gravity finish it off. In the Koronus Expanse, Thulean Magos Biologis of the Adeptus Mechanicus are known to liberally use this pattern of grapplehawk as both scouts in the field and aggressive sample "gatherers" of xenos life forms and other alien fauna, while at least a few outlandish bounty hunters—notably including the infamous Sanger Krin—have at one point employed a Falax to drag human prey back to their tender attentions.

### **Psyber-Eagle**
A truly exquisite status symbol, psyber-eagles are created as gifts from the Adeptus Mechanicus to the grandees of the Imperium, often as an honourific or reward for services rendered. These vat-grown raptors bear two heads in clear reference to the Imperial aquila, and to possess one is seen as an undeniable accord to the Imperium's ideals and aims. Within Calixis the Lord Sector Marius Hax himself is accompanied by a pair of psyber-eagles of prodigious size and splendour, gifts from the Mechanicus Fabricator-Lords of the Lathe System in recognition of his ascendance—and a tacit promise that he will not interfere in their affairs. The eagles circle above him in the vast vaulted halls of the Lucid Palace, their eyes those of their master scanning from on high.

### **Servo-Skulls**

An absolutely bewildering diversity of servo-skulls across the breadth of the Imperium—any task that a man might conceivably need an extra set of hands, eyes, or other tools for a servo-skull likely exists for it. Some more common varieties of servo-skulls are presented here.
#### **Guardian-Skull**
Designed with a reinforced structure and fitted with a single weapon system and targeting selection programs, guardian-skulls (also known as gun-skulls and dissector-skulls depending on their armament) are uncommon but still quite effective bodyguard devices. Their small size, tireless vigilance, and ability to hover in the shadows silently make them surprisingly discreet and efficient servants. Compared to larger combat servitors a guardian-skull is lacking in both firepower and durability, but possesses excellent mobility and can skulk about unseen in many environments. They're also certainly easier to transport when not in use and far less obtrusive in operation—handy when social considerations demand decorum that would not allow a more heavily armed attendant.

A guardian-skull may be commanded in the same way as a cyber-familiar would. Additionally, its hardwired combat algorithms allow it to be issued the following commands which it will carry out unerringly:

* **Protect:** The guardian-skull follows an assigned individual and will guard him or her by attacking anything or anyone that attacks or threatens them. An approximate "tolerance level" of what the guardian-skull will consider an attack or threat can be specified at this time, allowing the guardian-skull to disregard verbal threats, deliberate jostling, open-handed strikes, and so forth if the construct's master so wishes. The guardian-skull will either follow close by or hang back in an overwatch position at the master's choosing, and if instructed to do so will use the [Guardian](talents.md#guardian%20%28ascendant%20talent%29) Talent to intercept an attack that would otherwise have struck its assigned charge.
* **Search and Destroy:** The guardian-skull will methodically reconnoitre an indicated area and attack any creatures it detects which are not on a predetermined list of exceptions. Great care must be given with this instruction—"search and destroy everyone within this hab" would obviously include the armed gangers lurking within, but the guardian-skull will also ruthlessly terminate any other bystanders, animals (including any vermin it finds), and even the master's allies if they enter the hab while the instruction is still active.
* **Sentry:** The guardian-skull will ward a specified location, object, or some other defined perimeter, attacking anything that enters the warded area or attempts to interact with an indicated object. As with the previous command a predetermined list of exceptions can be provided when the command is issued and such beings will be allowed to pass by unmolested. Any time the warded area or object is violated the guardian-skull will use its voxplant to report the breach to its master or another assigned individual.

* **Dissector-Skull:** Articulated or serpentine limbs tipped with mono-edged blades are neatly folded up or coiled against this guardian-skull's chassis. When its combat algorithms are activated, either by command or through perceiving a threat, the dissector-skull deploys its razor-tipped appendages and swiftly transforms into a derangedly whirling blur of frenetic violence. The small size of the blades and the dissector-skull's low mass equate to strikes that are not especially deadly, but it attacks with terrifying speed that is notably difficult to defend against. Even a lightly-armoured opponent can be reduced to bloody ribbons in short order as the construct wildly flails about its target and lashes them with repeated slicing blows. The dissector-skull gains the [Swift Attack](talents.md#swift%20attack) Talent, and its in-built blades attack with the following profile: 1d5+2 R, Pen 2, [Flexible](galaxy%20of%20guns.md#flexible), [Tearing](galaxy%20of%20guns.md#tearing), [Unwieldy](galaxy%20of%20guns.md#unwieldy).
* **Gun-Skull:** One half of the gun-skull's chassis is given over to mounting a ranged weapon and its attendant ammunition supply while a targeting oculus fills one of its eye sockets on the other side. Conceivably any Pistol and some more compact Basic weapons could be fitted, but these must be acquired separately from the gun-skull itself, and the modifications to attach one to the gun-skull render it unusable by human hands. Note that a gun-skull has no provision for reloading its mounted weapon, and unless given instructions otherwise will attempt to return to its master or another designated charge to be reloaded when its ammunition supply is depleted. The gun-skull's targeting oculus, close communion between the skull's machine spirit and those of the mounted weapon, and the construct's relative stability make it a surprisingly capable marksman: it gains a +10 bonus to all Ballistic Skill Tests and suffers no penalties for firing at [Long or Extreme Range](combat.md#range).
#### **Harrier-Skull**
Mockingly referred to as "grapplemice" by the Kasballica and other well-heeled lowlives, this is not a single pattern of servo-skull insomuch as a category of improvised constructs that can act as highly effective decoys for grapplehawks. These are mainly constructed by reclaimators using the salvaged remains of damaged or defective servo-skulls, which are not terribly hard to find on most hive and forge worlds. The servo-skull's guidance cogitator is modified with a specific set of evasion patterns that are inexplicably irresistible to grapplehawks, while its gyros and anti-grav motor are deliberately overcharged to increase its manoeuvrability. This tends to rapidly burn out the already degraded power systems, but as a harrier-skull is really only intended to be used once this is of little consequence to their users. A lawbreaker, facing pursuit by a grapplehawk-equipped bounty hunter or Arbitrator, can release a harrier-skull which will begin to erratically fly about on its own without any further input from its owner. Something about the particular swooping and twisting flight of the harrier-skull has a good chance of triggering a grapplehawk's threat/targeting routines, sending the avian construct to attack the harrier-skull instead of its original target (i.e. the lawbreaker). The harrier-skull will move about randomly, attempting to evade the grapplehawk's attack until it is destroyed or its power systems burn out, by which time its owner has hopefully made their escape. Harrier-skulls are utterly despised by the Adeptus Arbites, and the reclaimators who manufacture them are likely to face some absolutely hideous fate if discovered by an aggrieved Arbitrator—forcible conversion into a bomb-disposal servitor being a particular favourite.

#### **Monotask-Skull**
As the name implies, this more common variety of servo-skull is programmed to assist its operator with a particular task and equipped with a variety of tools and gear to help carry that out. As they are intended to be able to remotely carry out instructions, monotask-skulls are generally equipped with a pict-relay that allows it to record what it sees and senses for later review—this information can also be relayed via a voxplant to a linked cogitator for "live" viewing. The construct can also simply report directly to its master an approximate transcription of what it perceives. Most are equipped with a basic set of extendable pincers to perform rudimentary manipulation of objects, and can generally be relied upon to at least be able to hold an object if instructed by their master to do so.

A monotask-skull may be commanded in the same way a cyber-familiar would, but its very simple machine spirit means it is generally only capable of executing instructions that are at least tangentially related to its intended function. An illuminator-skull might be able to pick up a small object and carry it across the room, but lacks the necessary scanners and programmatic routines to identify a specific individual to bring the object to.

* **Augur-skull:** Occasionally referred to by laymen as a "skull probe", an augur-skull is equipped with an [auspex](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#auspex/scanner) and a more powerful vox data relay array, allowing it to transmit its findings in real time to a linked cogitator, a microvox-equipped data-slate, or some other compatible receiver. An augur-skull may use its auspex in active mode by testing Awareness instead of Tech-Use as normal. This kind of servo-skull is frequently used for both maintenance and security purposes by the Adeptus Mechanicus and those with good ties to them. Augur-skulls can be sent into duct- and pipe-works in manufactora to inspect for damage and wear, and in many cases are nimble enough to fit into the inner workings of great machines (often while they're still operating!) to check for same. They are also equally effective as perimeter sentries for highly secured facilities, tirelessly skulking about and searching for trespassers and other scofflaws.
* **Courier-skull:** This servo-skull primarily functions as a messenger, but can also be used to transport and deliver small objects over a great distance. A gyro-orienter and terrain-following scanners give this construct exceptionally reliable ability to determine its relative location in regards to where it expects its recipient to be. On arriving, a specialised medispex implant is used to verify the identity of its intended recipient, checking the target's biologic identifiers against a programmed sample or description when it is sent out. A courier-skull is fitted with a narrow cargo compartment large enough to fit a few scrolls, a data-slate, or a book of reasonable thickness, and its manipulators are typically nimble enough to retrieve the parcel and "hand it" to its recipient upon delivery. Instead of a physical missive, a courier-slate can also deliver a vox recording dictated by its sender. [Good Craftsmanship](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#craftsmanship) examples are fitted with a holo-projector system, allowing for a rather ostentatious display of a life-size vid-capture of the sender's message instead.
* **Illuminator-skull:** Possibly the simplest common style of servo-skull, the illuminator-skull exists purely to act as a mobile light source for its master. Depending on the particulars of its design, it may be equipped with a [glow-globe](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#glow-globe/lamp%20pack) for omnidirectional lighting or a powerful [stab-light](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#stab-light) to illuminate point targets. More rarely, an elaborate metallic brazier fed by a compressed fuel tank can be mounted to the top of the skull itself, creating an extremely dramatic (if whimsical) appearance. This style is especially favoured by members of the Ecclesiarchy, and "servo-braziers" are common sights in many of the Imperium's grander churches. Regardless of their particular method of light-bringing, an illuminator-skull can be instructed to follow along its master or another designated individual in order to light their path, remain stationary to illuminate an area or object, or follow a predetermined route through an area to routinely light up its more important features.
* **Loudhailer-skull:** This construct is fitted with a relatively powerful vox emitter, typically mounted under its maxilla or in a smaller pair on either side of the skull. Loudhailer-skulls are commonly used as a form of public address or annunciation, replaying recorded messages of interest or importance that can be heard over the din of large crowds or operating machinery. Less commonly—although perhaps of greater interest to an Acolyte—it can also be used to remotely transmit and amplify the voice of its master, giving anyone with a connected voxlink a booming enunciation that can be blared from a considerable distance away if so desired.
* **Medicae-skull:** This servo-skull is commonly employed as an assistant to a human medicae or chirurgeon, but is also programmed with sufficient healing and care routines to provide competent first aid on its own. A battery of small manipulators dangle below the skull which can retrieve all the typical tools used in a [medikit](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#medikit) from perfectly fitted slots in the back of the chassis, while a hard-wired [diagnostor](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#diagnostor/medispex) implant can be used to diagnose ongoing conditions and afflictions, which the medicae-skull can recite in a grating monotone voice. Between its in-built programming, medikit, and diagnostor, the medicae-skull can attempt [Medicae](skills.md#medicae) Skill Tests as if it had an Intelligence of 55. A medicae-skull can use the First Aid and Diagnose special rules, but not Extended Care (although it can provide [Assistance](tests.md#assistance) to another trained medicae in this).
* **Utility-skull:** A utility-skull is equipped with a battery of hard-wired tools and several manipulators that allow it to function as if equipped with a [combi-tool](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#combi-tool). A utility-skull's programmatic routines do not allow it to make [Tech-Use](skills.md#tech-use) Tests on its own, but when working with Imperial technology it is capable of automatically passing any Tech-Use Tests whose difficulty is **Ordinary (+10)** or easier. Anything more difficult is simply beyond the utility-skull's ability to perform on its own. While it is thus fully capable of performing basic tasks and coaxing machine spirits into action, the main purpose of the utility-skull is to act as a second set of "hands" for its master. A utility-skull can autonomously assist its master or another assigned user, reducing the difficulty of Tech-Use and [Trade](skills.md#trade) (Technomat) Tests by one step. Alternatively, the operator can make use of the utility-skull's mobility to fit into constrained spaces and hard to reach areas that would otherwise be problematic to access, effectively allowing him to make Tech-Use or Trade (Technomat) Tests without having to physically handle the object or artifice in question. The utility-skull can also be used remotely if linked to a cogitator or microvox-equipped data-slate, although the operator suffers a –5 penalty to any Tests made using the utility-skull in this way.
#### **Servo-Hunter**
Servo-hunters are a broad class of relatively inexpensive and often handmade constructs built to aid enforcers, bounty hunters, and other trackers in pursuing their quarries. Unlike a true servo-skull, a servo-hunter is usually constructed using the skull of a predatory animal—typically a canine or some similar beast—as these are thought to benefit the machine spirit's ability to pursue prey whilst remaining loyal to their masters. Most are built by reclaimators or are the products of low-hive scav-workshops, often using salvaged guidance cogitators and suspensors from larger servitors or discarded servo-skulls, and orthodox tech-priests of the Adeptus Mechanicus tend to take a dim view of their usage. Despite this there seems to be no shortage of demand from all sorts of those who hunt other men for duty, honour, or profit, and indeed there are many amongst the Malfian Bloodsworn who dismiss any bounty hunter who doesn't possess one of these imminently useful constructs. When activated, a servo-hunter can be instructed to investigate a given area and search for targets there which match an indicated parameter. A servo-hunter's very rudimentary machine spirit and lack of sophisticated scanning sensors limit its selective ability, so while one could be instructed to "search for the man in this storehouse", it would likely not be able to pick out a single individual in a crowd unless the target had a very distinctive appearance. Once the target is located the servo-hunter will give an audible alarm of some kind and will doggedly pursue the target, stymieing the target's ability to hide from or otherwise evade the servo-hunter's owner. It can also be used as a reasonably effective perimeter sentry if it is instructed to monitor a given area and issue an alarm if anyone approaches.

## **Upgrades**

This section covers upgrades and modifications specific to familiars, creating cyber- and psyber-creatures from flesh-and-blood animals, and other relevant miscellany.
#### **Berserker-Chip**
Also known as "lobos" and "butcher-jobs", these devices are surgically embedded in the creature's brain and adrenal system and can turn an ordinarily docile cyber-familiar into a ravaging monster at a moment's notice. When activated, the chip sends it into a murderous rage, backed by a heavy dose of locally-synthesised combat drugs pumped into its remaining organic tissues. Depending on the creator's preferences these chips are activated either by a command word or signal sent directly to the beast.

Creatures under the influence of a berserker-chip gain the [Frenzy](talents.md#frenzy) Talent, which may be triggered instantaneously by a codeword or signal from its master. Aside from the usual benefits of the Frenzy Talent the cyber-familiar may also ignore the effects of [Characteristic Damage](injury%20and%20healing.md#characteristic%20damage) and [Critical Damage](injury%20and%20healing.md#critical%20damage) as well, fighting on through broken bones and severed limbs as best it can (an instant death effect from the Head or Body location will still end its rampage, however). The chip is deactivated again by a command word or signal, after which the creature gains 1d5 levels of [Fatigue](injury%20and%20healing.md#fatigue) and will begin to suffer any other effects of injury it may have sustained.

A berserker-chip is far from a perfect form of weaponised natural fury, however. Each time the cyber-familiar goes into a Frenzy there is a 10% chance the implant will malfunction and refuse to deactivate even when the command is given. This roll should be made in secret by the GM. If the berserker-chip malfunctions the cyber-familiar will remain in a Frenzied state, ignoring any further instructions from its master and continuing to attack an enemies present, after which it will turn its attentions to bystanders, allies, or even its master if he's unfortunate enough. Unless it is destroyed first the creature will continue to violently rampage for the next few hours until it dies from overexertion and shock.
#### **Constructer Interface**
This rare and esoteric implant was originally developed for Cyber-mastiff Handlers of the Adeptus Arbites, but is a great boon for anyone who is heavily invested in the creation or operation of cyber-constructs or creatures. A constructer interface allows its bearer to issue commands to cyber-familiars and other constructs via vox, and if the creature is so equipped with the relevant implants and augments, receive pict and vox data transmissions from the creature for immediate review. Servitors and servo-skulls can also be patched into a constructer interface, allowing the bearer to receive information from the construct in question. A servitor or servo-skull may or may not be able to be issued commands by the bearer depending on its construction.

A constructer interface can be uplinked to up to five separate cyber-creatures or servitors that are owned by (or at least delegated to) its bearer. [Common Craftsmanship](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#craftsmanship) interfaces can issue commands to two creatures at once, while a Good Craftsmanship example can simultaneously command three creatures. The bearer may issue instructions as if using the [Command](skills.md#command) Skill to order around a subordinate, although the bearer uses their [Tech-Use](skills.md#tech-use) Skill for the purposes of making the relevant Tests. The interface has a maximum effective range of about one kilometre, similar to a micro-bead, and its range may be impeded by interference in much the same way.

Certain creatures which no longer possess the Bestial Trait, such as heavily-augmented cyber-mastiffs and most grapple-hawks, may be issued a "control pattern" by the constructer interface. This uploads a series of manoeuvres and protocols to the construct which are imminently suitable for various situations, effectively improving the creature's abilities to carry out it's master's will beyond what it would normally be able to perform. Uploading a pattern into a given construct is a Full Action and requires a successful **Ordinary (+10) Tech-Use Test**. Until it is issued a new pattern, the creature will gain one of the following based on which pattern is assigned:

* **Attack:** +10 Weapon Skill
* **Restrain:** +20 Weapon Skill when attempting to [Grapple](combat.md#grapple%20%28full%20action%29)
* **Harry:** +10 [Dodge](skills.md#dodge), Dodge Skill Training
* **Guard:** +10 Perception, [Awareness](skills.md#awareness) Skill Training
* **Seek:** +10 Perception, [Tracking](skills.md#tracking) Skill Training
* **Pursue:** +1 Agility Bonus, Accustomed to Crowds Trait
	* **Accustomed to Crowds:** Crowds do not count as [Difficult Terrain](movement.md#movement%20and%20the%20environment). When [Running](combat.md#run%20%28full%20action%29) or [Charging](combat.md#charge%20%28full%20action%29) through a dense crowd the creature takes no penalty to its Agility Test to keep on its feet.
#### **Cyber-Creature (Upgrade)**
Any creature with the [Bestial](traits.md#bestial) Trait of Scrawny Size or less may be upgraded into a cyber-familiar. Certain larger animals—often canids and similar beasts—may be able to become a cyber-creature, though compatibility with the technology is far from guaranteed. Such adjudications should be handled on a case by case basis at the GM's discretion. An animal becoming a cyber-creature gains the following modifications:

- The creature gains the [Machine](traits.md#machine%20%28X%29) (2) Trait and +2 Wounds.
- The creature's natural attacks no longer count as [Primitive](galaxy%20of%20guns.md#primitive).
- The creature gains +5 to its Strength and Toughness Characteristics each and –5 to its Agility.

For very heavily augmented cyber-creatures, or those who are mostly or entirely mechanical in construction, the creature [increases its Machine Trait](familiars.md#machine%20trait) to between 3 to 6, gains the [Fearless](talents.md#fearless) Talent and loses the [Bestial](traits.md#bestial) Trait. Such creatures make for implacable combatants and guardians, but act far more like a servitor than an animal. A cyber-creature without the [Bestial](traits.md#bestial) Trait will follow any instructions given regardless of danger or threats, but lacks any of the instinctive behaviours of an unaugmented animal and will only act at its master's direction. Commonly referred to as a cyber-construct, or "hardcase" in some precincts, a creature so augmented will unfailingly follow any commands given, but may completely ignore a target it was not told to investigate or brutally attack bystanders (or allies!) if not given clearly exclusionary instructions when set loose.

If a cyber-familiar ever suffers from a failure of four or more degrees on a Willpower Test or suffers [Critical Damage](injury%20and%20healing.md#critical%20damage) to the head, there is a 50% chance for its programming to break down catastrophically and a debased form of its organic mind will take over. This breaks the master's ability to control or give instructions to the cyber-familiar, which will then either revert to an animalistic state or simply go mad and viciously attack everything and everyone around it until it is destroyed. The latter is more likely with heavily augmented familiars or those which no longer possess the Bestial Trait.
#### **Augmetic Implant**
Owing to their sturdy construction, being either partly or wholly machine, a cyber-creature can receive [various augmetics](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#implants) and the necessary cortical controllers to use them in much the same way a human can. Creating an implant suitable for a cyber-creature costs twice as much as one intended for a human, while the Availability remains the same. Not all implants or bionics are necessarily worthwhile (or even usable) by a cyber-creature—while a cyber-mastiff could conceivably have scribe-tines installed in its forelegs, none would ever be intelligent enough to use them for their intended purpose. Consult the GM for which implants may or may not be suitable for installing in a cyber-creature.
#### **Cortex Implant**
The mind of a cyber-creature is already heavily augmented, laced with neural interface circuitry and bio-control logis stacks to that enable it to operate its various bionic components and slave it to its master's will. Further cortical implants can, at some expense, endow a cyber-creature with additional powers and capabilities that it would not otherwise possess. Each cortex implant can add one [Skill](skills.md) or [Talent](talents.md) to the cyber-creature's existing ones. [Good Craftsmanship](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#craftsmanship) cortex implants can grant a Skill at a +10 [Mastery](skills.md#skill%20mastery), while Best Craftsmanship versions improve to +20 Mastery. Note that the cyber-creature must be able to meet any prerequisites that a Talent normally requires, and unless the creature already possesses the Skill in question, only [Basic Skills](skills.md#basic%20skills) may be added by means of a cortex implant. Some Skills and Talents may be impossible to use with a cyber-creature—this is left to the GM's discretion.

Improving a cyber-familiar with cortical implants does have one notable limitation. The comparably small brains of most creatures only have a fairly small amount of grey matter available for augmentation, and consequently each implant must be installed with great care and often "creative" arrangements of the circuitry. The cost of each cortex implant increases by 50% from the last to reflect the growing difficulty of adding more and more implants to a rapidly diminishing organ.
#### **Machine Trait**
The overall durability and ruggedness of a cyber-creature can be further enhanced by replacing an increasing amount of its remaining biologics with mechanical components. The more of the creature's body that is replaced with alloy analogues and electrically-motivated fibre bundles the harder it will be to put down with blades and gunfire, making a stalwart cyber-creature even more of a reliable companion in combat. Replacing most, or indeed all, of an animal's flesh does tend to significantly alter its psychology, however, and owners would be well-advised to understand that a heavily augmented cyber-creature will act more like a servitor than the animal it once was.

Cyber-creatures with a [Machine Trait](traits.md#machine%20%28X%29) value of 3 or greater lose the [Bestial](traits.md#bestial) Trait and gain the [Fearless](talents.md#fearless) Talent. A cyber-creature without the Bestial Trait will follow any instructions given regardless of danger or threats, but lacks any of the instinctive behaviours of an unaugmented animal and will only act at its master's direction.

The expense and difficulty of replacing more and more of a creature's original body with machine components does tend to escalate rapidly the more heavily augmented the creature becomes. The cost of each increase in the creature's Machine Trait increases by 50% from the last to reflect the cost of further systems and in-built life sustainers which must be added to keep the creature's remaining flesh and bone alive.
#### **Psyber-Creature (Upgrade)**
Most creatures with the [Bestial](traits.md#bestial) Trait and a [Size](traits.md#size%20%28X%29) of Scrawny or less can be modified into a psyber-creature. The owner must also acquire a [psyber-lure](familiars.md#psyber-lure) implant and have it surgically implanted to make full use of the creature's abilities. An animal becoming a psyber-creature gains the following modifications:

* The creature gains +5 to its Intelligence and Willpower Characteristics each.
* The creature gains 1 Wound.

By focusing on his psyber-lure, the owner can sense everything his psy-bonded familiar does, and with practice can perfectly control the creature's movements and actions as if it was an extension of his own body. In combat the owner may divide his Actions between his own body and the psyber-creature's in order to maintain direct control. While it is being controlled the psyber-creature makes Tests using the owner's mental Characteristics (Intelligence, Willpower, and Fellowship), and the owner is free to attempt any Action using the psyber-creature's body it might reasonably be able to perform. Controlling the psyber-creature in this way is obviously constrained to the particular creature's anatomy—the owner could easily attempt a [Lip Reading](skills.md#lip%20reading) or [Scrutiny](skills.md#scrutiny) Test using his psyber-eagle's eyes and ears, and while it may be able to attempt a [Search](skills.md#search) Test to rummage through the contents of a room using its beak and talons, it likely could not use [Security](skills.md#security) to pick a lock. Likewise, operating a cogitator using [Tech-Use](skills.md#tech-use) would be almost certainly impossible.

The range at which a character can influence a psy-bonded creature is equal to their Willpower Bonus in kilometres, although local conditions might limit this drastically. Areas with dense vox communication, operating macroforges or plasma reactors, void shields, or just heavy, thick walls can shorten the psyber-lure's effective radius to less than a few hundred or even dozens of metres per point of the master's Willpower Bonus.

A creature may be given both the [Cyber-Creature](familiars.md#cyber-creature%20%28upgrade%29) and Psyber-Creature upgrades to make an extremely versatile, if bespoke and costly, piece of techno-artifice that any master would be proud to own.

**Special:** A character with a Psy Rating of 2 or more may treat his psyber-familiar as a [Psy-Focus](psychic%20powers.md#psychic-foci). At the GM's discretion he could also extend the effects of certain psychic powers to affect the psyber-familiar as well, including those with a Range of "You", although this increases the power's Psychic Threshold by 5.
#### **Psyber-Lure**
This marvellous piece of technology is a vital component of controlling a psyber-creature. Often taking the form of a finger-sized piece of glossy alloy, it must be implanted in the master's body where it can be wired into his nervous system—the back of the neck or side of the head is a common location. This follows the same rules as [attaching any other augmetic](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#attaching%20an%20augmetic), and requires a successful Medicae Test as if it were a [cerebral plug](tools%20of%20the%20trade.md#cerebral%20plug). Through a lengthy rite that must be performed by a member of the Adeptus Mechanicus the psyber-lure and a psyber-creature may be linked together, with the lure acting as a transmitter for the master's instructions and a receiver for the psyber-creature's senses. Once bound in this way the master and his psyber-familiar are indefinitely linked, and only the master's death, destruction of the psyber-lure or the familiar, or another Mechanicus ritual can permanently sever them. The bearer of the psyber-lure can have any number of psyber-creatures linked to it, but can only control and perceive the senses of one psyber-creature at any given moment.
